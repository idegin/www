Buy the tool first, and you will spend the next year forcing your business to fit software that was never designed for it. We do the opposite.

## Start with how work really flows

Documentation describes how a company is *supposed* to work. Reality is different — approvals route through a specific person's inbox, a spreadsheet quietly holds the whole operation together, and the real bottleneck is nowhere on the org chart.

So we begin with interviews and shadowing, not a product roadmap. We learn:

- how departments actually collaborate
- where work stalls and why
- which tasks are repetitive, rules-based, and ready to automate
- which decisions require human judgement and must stay that way

## Map before you build

Once we can see the operation end to end, the automation candidates become obvious — and so do the traps. A process that looks automatable often hides an exception that only a person can resolve. Mapping surfaces it before it becomes a production incident.

## Then, and only then, design

With a real operational picture, we design the AI employees, agents, and software around your processes — not the other way around. The result is technology that feels like it was built for your business, because it was.

The order matters. Business before technology is not a slogan; it is the difference between automation that sticks and automation that gets switched off.
